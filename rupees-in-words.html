Nepal counts in twos, not threes

English groups digits in threes: thousand, million, billion. Nepal groups the last three digits, then twos: thousand, lakh, crore, arab. The same figure is written 12,34,567 here and 1,234,567 abroad, and read "twelve lakh thirty four thousand five hundred sixty seven".

  • 1 thousand = 1,000
  • 1 lakh = 1,00,000 (100 thousand)
  • 1 crore = 1,00,00,000 (100 lakh)
  • 1 arab = 1,00,00,00,000 (100 crore)
  • 1 kharab = 100 arab
  • 1 shankha = 100 kharab

Paisa. Anything after the decimal point is read as paisa and rounded to the nearest one, so 5.999 becomes six rupees. "Only" is added at the end so the amount cannot be extended after it is written.

Limit. Up to 15 digits before the decimal point (99 shankha 99 kharab and below).

The Nepali number words (1 to 99 are irregular) are held as a fixed list. Check unusual values, such as 54, 59, 69, 79, 89 and 99, against your bank's own wording before writing a cheque.
